I
The Petrodollar — What Actually Happened in June 2024

First, a necessary precision: there was no single "petrodollar agreement" that expired in 2024. This matters enormously because misunderstanding the mechanism leads to misunderstanding the risk. What actually expired in June 2024 was the Joint US-Saudi Economic Commission — a 50-year institutional framework established in 1974. The commission did not contain a formal requirement to price oil in dollars. What it did was institutionalise the recycling of petrodollars — Saudi oil revenue → invested into US Treasuries → sustaining dollar demand. The underlying practice was an informal arrangement revealed by Bloomberg via a 2016 FOIA request.

The more accurate framing of what changed: Saudi Arabia, by not renewing, signalled that it no longer feels bound by the informal dollar-oil pricing norm. It can now sell oil in yuan, euros, yen, or ruble without institutional constraint. Whether and how quickly it does so is a geopolitical choice, not an automatic mechanism. This is a slow-burning structural shift, not a binary switch.

II
Saudi-China Realignment — The Real Architecture

The 24-deal Saudi-China energy and infrastructure signing in April 2026 is not an isolated event — it is the visible capstone of a decade-long structural pivot. China already accounts for roughly 25% of Saudi crude exports. The architecture of the new relationship includes:

Yuan oil settlement Aramco-Sinopec JVs mBridge CBDC project Vision 2030 + BRI overlap CATL battery / EV supply chains Huawei 5G infrastructure

The mBridge CBDC project is the most structurally significant. It is a multi-central-bank digital currency platform enabling instant cross-border settlements without SWIFT — meaning sanctions evasion becomes technologically simple. Saudi Arabia joined mBridge in 2023 alongside China, UAE, Hong Kong, and Thailand. The IMF, Federal Reserve, and ECB are all observing members — watching anxiously. mBridge, if fully deployed, would represent the first viable alternative payment rail to SWIFT since Bretton Woods. It does not replace the dollar immediately — but it creates the infrastructure for a world where dollar primacy in trade finance is optional rather than mandatory.

The UAE leaving OPEC in late April 2026 compounds this. The UAE has expanded ADNOC production capacity to target 5 million barrels/day by 2027 — well beyond its OPEC quota — while simultaneously negotiating currency swap lines with the US Treasury. It is hedging between the dollar order and the emerging yuan order. This is not ideological alignment with China; it is transactional sovereignty maximisation.

III
America's War Calculus — Dollar Defence or Debt Camouflage?

Your framing — that the US is using geopolitical confrontation to manufacture a dollar-supportive crisis — has genuine intellectual lineage. The historical pattern is clear: oil shocks drive capital into dollar-denominated safe-haven assets (Treasuries), boosting Treasury demand precisely when the US most needs it. The 1973 Yom Kippur War, the Gulf War, and the Iraq War all had this effect, intentionally or not.

The Iran war context of 2025–2026 fits the same pattern. But here is why the gambit is structurally weaker than in prior cycles: Ships passing through Hormuz have at times paid tolls to Iran in Bitcoin. Iran sells oil to China in yuan. India is reportedly buying Iranian oil in yuan. The war is simultaneously strengthening Iran-China economic ties — the very alliance the US is trying to weaken. Every sanctions regime the US applies accelerates de-dollarisation, not the reverse. The 2022 Russia sanctions (freezing $300bn in FX reserves) was the seminal moment — it proved to every central bank in the world that dollar-denominated reserves can be weaponised. Gold cannot.

The US Debt Trap — The Arithmetic of No Good Options
$40T
Total federal debt — 125% of GDP. In 1980 when Volcker hiked to 20%, it was 33% of GDP. The "Volcker option" is now mathematically impossible.
$1.1T
Annual interest payments on US debt — exceeding the entire defence budget. Every 1% rise in rates adds ~$400bn/year to the interest bill within 3 years as debt rolls over.
$2T
US Treasury must borrow $2 trillion in 2026 alone just to keep functioning — more than $166 billion every month. Foreign buyers (China, Japan, Gulf states) have been net sellers of Treasuries since 2022.
-25%
Dollar purchasing power loss since 2018. The dollar has lost roughly one quarter of its purchasing power in 8 years — a pace that, if sustained, implies a 50% loss by 2034.
57%
Dollar's share of global FX reserves — heading towards its historic floor. If it falls below 50%, the feedback loop of declining Treasury demand → higher yields → higher deficits → more money printing accelerates geometrically.

The US faces what economists call Triffin's Dilemma — the fundamental contradiction of being both the world's reserve currency issuer and a sovereign state with its own economic interests. To supply enough dollars for global trade, the US must run perpetual current account deficits. But those deficits eventually erode the dollar's credibility. The system is self-defeating by construction. It always ends. The question is how — gradually, or catastrophically.

IV
The US Bitcoin Gambit — Genius or Desperation?

Trump's Strategic Bitcoin Reserve (March 2025) and the GENIUS Act (stablecoin legislation, July 2025) are the most underappreciated macro moves of the current cycle. Here is the logic:

2025 — Strategic Bitcoin Reserve
US holds 328,372 BTC (~$30B at current prices)
The largest known state holder of Bitcoin in the world. Executive order established the reserve using already-forfeited BTC — cost-neutral, no new purchases. Signals intent without fiscal commitment.
July 2025 — GENIUS Act signed
Stablecoins must be backed by US Treasury bills
This is the masterstroke. Every dollar of stablecoin issued worldwide requires a dollar of short-term US government debt as collateral. If stablecoins grow to $5–10 trillion (plausible by 2030), that is $5–10 trillion of captive demand for US Treasuries — potentially replacing the petrodollar recycling mechanism entirely with a cryptodollar recycling mechanism.
2026 — Adoption accelerating
30% of all Bitcoin held by 216 centralised entities
Corporations, banks, ETF firms, hedge funds, and sovereign wealth funds are hoarding Bitcoin. Strategy (MicroStrategy) holds ~3% of all BTC. Bitcoin is paradoxically becoming highly centralised — the opposite of its original design.
Near-term — Czech National Bank
First major central bank to purchase BTC (~$1M test position)
Deutsche Bank projects more central banks will hold Bitcoin alongside gold by end of decade. The ECB categorically refuses — calling Bitcoin's fair value "still zero." The global split on Bitcoin as reserve asset is widening.

The GENIUS Act is the real "way out" the US is betting on — not Bitcoin price appreciation. The mechanism: dollar-backed stablecoins issued by US banks/tech giants proliferate globally, particularly in countries with unstable local currencies (which is most of the developing world). This creates massive structural demand for US Treasuries as stablecoin collateral. It extends dollar dominance through crypto rails rather than petrodollar rails. It is brilliant if it works. The risk: if China deploys mBridge at scale with yuan-backed CBDCs before US stablecoins gain critical mass, the race is lost.

VII
The Real Endgame — What the Smart Money Knows

The honest synthesis — the one that macro legends like Luke Groman, Ray Dalio, Stanley Druckenmiller, and Paul Tudor Jones are all converging on — is this: the world is not transitioning to a new single reserve currency. It is transitioning to a multi-asset reserve system. Gold as the neutral, non-sovereign foundation. Bitcoin as the digital/programmable complement. Dollar-backed stablecoins as the transaction medium for global trade (the "plumbing"). And potentially an SDR-style basket for central bank settlement.

"Bitcoin and gold are going to go up versus virtually all other commodities, many other assets, as we move away from a debt reserve system."
Luke Groman, macro analyst — cited by multiple institutional funds as a foundational thesis

The specific insight you raised — that America is underwater and trying to swim by engineering crisis — is partially correct but incomplete. The US is not engineering crisis; it is struggling to prevent crisis while simultaneously trying to retain the enormous privilege of issuing the world's reserve currency. That privilege — the "exorbitant privilege" (de Gaulle's phrase) — means the US can run permanent deficits, fund wars, and sustain domestic consumption beyond its production capacity, all because the world needs dollars. Losing it would be the largest structural adjustment in American history. No US administration will willingly cede it.

The Bitcoin-as-escape-hatch thesis has some truth in it, but the mechanism is indirect: the US is not betting that Bitcoin replaces the dollar — it is betting that dollar-backed stablecoins on crypto rails replace dollar-denominated SWIFT transactions, thereby extending dollar dominance into the crypto era. Bitcoin's role is to be a neutral hard asset that the US also holds, so it doesn't "lose" in a world where Bitcoin appreciates against all fiat.

The Macro Thesis in One Paragraph
We are living through the slowest-moving financial revolution in modern history — the end of the pure dollar reserve system that has governed the world since 1971. It is not a single event. It is a decades-long transition being accelerated by four simultaneous forces: American fiscal unsustainability (the debt trap), geopolitical multipolarity (Saudi-China, petroyuan, mBridge), technological disruption (crypto rails replacing SWIFT), and the Basel III re-monetisation of gold. Gold is the clearest beneficiary because it is the only asset that all three competing powers — the US, China, and the neutral bloc — can agree holds value. Bitcoin is the wild card that benefits from the same debasement thesis but with far higher volatility and a still-unresolved governance question. The dollar survives in a diminished form, its dominance extended by stablecoin infrastructure rather than petrodollar recycling. The world order that emerges is not a Chinese century or an American century — it is a multipolar monetary system in which no single asset or currency dominates, and in which hard assets (gold first, Bitcoin second) serve as the neutral settlement layer between competing blocs. In this world, being early to gold and Bitcoin — and underweight long-duration fiat assets — is not speculation. It is the most historically-informed portfolio construction available.
